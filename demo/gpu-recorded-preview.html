ANSWERED · 14917 ms observed

A market order executes at the best available price in the order book, ensuring execution but not guaranteeing a specific price. A limit order specifies a maximum buying price or minimum selling price and only executes if a matching price is available at or better than the set level. A limit order does not guarantee execution and may remain unfilled if no matching liquidity is present. Evidence: DEMO01:c0001, DEMO02:c0001, DEMO06:c0001.

DEMO01:c0001, DEMO02:c0001, DEMO06:c0001
DEMO01:c0001 · original note

A market order prioritizes execution at available order-book prices. A limit order sets a maximum buying price or minimum selling price. A limit order can remain unfilled; it does not guarantee immediate execution.

DEMO02:c0001 · original note

A stop-limit order has a trigger price and a limit price. Reaching the trigger activates a limit order; it does not guarantee execution. If no matching liquidity is available at the limit price or better, the order can remain unfilled.

DEMO06:c0001 · original note

A maker adds resting liquidity to an order book. A taker consumes existing liquidity. A limit order that crosses available opposing orders can act as a taker; the order label alone does not determine its liquidity role.
